# Day 2 – Understanding AI Roles and the AI Ecosystem

**Date:** 23 July 2026

## Today's Goal
Today's session focused on understanding the AI ecosystem, learning about the role of a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), exploring Go-To-Market (GTM) strategies, AI Economics, and revisiting the core AI concepts introduced on the first day.

## What I Learned

### Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)
Today, I learned about the role of a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE). An FDE works closely with clients to understand their business problems and build customized AI solutions. Unlike traditional software engineers, FDEs combine technical skills with strong communication and problem-solving abilities. They bridge the gap between customer requirements and engineering teams, ensuring that AI products solve real-world challenges effectively.

### Go-To-Market (GTM)
I learned that a Go-To-Market (GTM) strategy is the plan for introducing a product or service to the market. It involves identifying the target audience, understanding customer needs, deciding pricing, selecting marketing channels, and planning how the product will reach users successfully. A strong GTM strategy plays a key role in a product's success.

### AI Economics
Today's session also introduced AI Economics. I understood that AI is not only about innovation but also about creating business value. Organizations use AI to automate repetitive tasks, reduce costs, improve efficiency, enhance customer experiences, and make better data-driven decisions. AI has become an important investment because of its ability to increase productivity and generate long-term value.

## Revisiting Previous Topics

### Agentic AI
We explored Agentic AI in greater detail. I learned that AI agents can independently plan, reason, use tools, and perform tasks to achieve specific goals with minimal human intervention. This makes them suitable for handling complex workflows and decision-making processes.

### Generative AI
I gained a deeper understanding of Generative AI and how it creates new content such as text, images, code, audio, and videos. It uses advanced machine learning models trained on large datasets and is transforming industries like education, healthcare, software development, and content creation.

### Large Language Models (LLMs)
We discussed how Large Language Models (LLMs) process natural language, understand context, and generate meaningful responses. Models such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude are examples of LLMs that assist with writing, coding, summarization, translation, and problem-solving.

### Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
I learned more about Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). Instead of relying only on pre-trained knowledge, RAG retrieves relevant information from external sources before generating a response. This improves the accuracy, relevance, and reliability of AI-generated answers, especially when working with domain-specific knowledge.

### Playwright Library
We also revised the Playwright library. I learned that it is a browser automation framework used for web testing and automation. It supports multiple browsers and allows developers to automate tasks such as filling forms, navigating web pages, clicking buttons, taking screenshots, and testing web applications efficiently.
