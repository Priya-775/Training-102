## What I Learned

### 1. Prompt Engineering
The major topic of today's session was Prompt Engineering. I learned that the quality of an AI model's response depends heavily on how the prompt is written. A well-structured prompt provides clear instructions, context, and expected output, helping the AI generate more accurate and useful responses.

A good prompt usually includes:
- Clear objective
- Relevant context
- Specific instructions
- Expected output format
- Constraints (if any)

For example:

**Simple Prompt**
> Explain Artificial Intelligence.

**Better Prompt**
> Explain Artificial Intelligence in simple language for a beginner. Include real-life examples and keep the explanation within 200 words.

This comparison helped me understand how prompt quality directly influences the quality of AI-generated responses.

### 2. Wikipedia as a Knowledge Source
Today's session also discussed Wikipedia and its role in AI research.

I learned that Wikipedia is one of the world's largest open-source knowledge platforms containing articles on millions of topics. It is useful for understanding concepts, getting background information, and exploring unfamiliar subjects.

However, since anyone can edit Wikipedia, it is always a good practice to verify important information using official or trusted sources.

### 3. Anthropic
I was introduced to Anthropic, an AI company that develops advanced language models under the Claude family.

I learned that Anthropic focuses on building AI systems that are:
- Helpful
- Honest
- Safe

Their approach emphasizes responsible AI development and reducing harmful or misleading outputs. It was interesting to learn how different AI companies focus not only on model performance but also on AI safety and ethics.

---

### 4. Kettle
Today's session also included an introduction to Kettle.

I learned that Kettle is a data integration and ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) tool used for collecting data from different sources, transforming it into the required format, and loading it into databases or data warehouses.

It helps automate data-processing workflows and is commonly used in data engineering projects where large amounts of data need to be cleaned and organized before analysis.
