# What I Learned

## 1. Knowledge Matrix

The Knowledge Matrix is a framework used to organize and understand knowledge based on different levels of awareness and expertise. It helps identify what we already know, what we need to learn, and areas that require further exploration.

The four categories are:

### Known Known
Information that we already know and understand well.

**Example:**
- Basic Python programming
- What is Generative AI

### Known Unknown
Things we know exist but have not learned yet.

**Example:**
- Building AI agents
- Fine-tuning language models

### Unknown Known
Knowledge that we possess but may not consciously recognize until needed.

**Example:**
Problem-solving skills gained from previous experiences.

### Unknown Unknown
Completely new concepts that we are not even aware exist.

These are often discovered through continuous learning and experimentation.

## 2. GUMS–FUMS Matrix

Today's session introduced the GUMS–FUMS Matrix as a way of evaluating ideas, solutions, or technologies from different perspectives.

Although the exact interpretation can vary depending on the organization, the main idea is to compare different approaches by analyzing:

- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Future potential
- Practical usefulness

This structured thinking helps teams make better technical and business decisions.

## 3. Omnivore Learning

I learned about the concept of being an **Omnivore Learner**.

An omnivore learner does not depend on a single source of knowledge. Instead, they learn from multiple resources such as:

- Books
- Research papers
- Documentation
- AI tools
- Videos
- Blogs
- Practical projects
- Community discussions

This approach develops a broader understanding and improves problem-solving skills.

One important lesson from today's session was:

> "The best developers don't rely on one resource—they learn from many."

## 4. Quantum Computing

Today's session introduced the basics of Quantum Computing.

Unlike classical computers, which use **bits** (0 or 1), quantum computers use **qubits**.

A qubit can exist in multiple states simultaneously due to a property called **superposition**.

This enables quantum computers to solve certain complex problems much faster than traditional computers.

Potential applications include:

- Drug discovery
- Cryptography
- Financial modeling
- Artificial Intelligence
- Optimization problems
- Scientific research

Although quantum computing is still evolving, it has the potential to transform many industries.

      Bit                           Qubit
   0 or 1 only             0, 1, or both (Superposition)

## 5. Quantum Entanglement

One of the most fascinating concepts introduced today was **Quantum Entanglement**.

Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon where two or more particles become connected in such a way that the state of one particle is related to the state of another, even if they are separated by a large distance.

This concept is important because it enables:

- Quantum communication
- Quantum teleportation research
- Secure quantum cryptography
- Faster quantum computations

Although it is difficult to visualize using everyday experiences, quantum entanglement is one of the fundamental principles behind quantum computing.
